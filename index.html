NOW SHOWING · 1980 – 2024

Hollywood:
A Closed Club?

Forty years of who works with whom, what the stories are about, and whether audiences notice.

DTU 02805 Social Graphs & Interactions · Final project · Fall 2026

THE PITCH

Research question

Over the last four decades, has Hollywood's collaboration network become more closed (more clustered, more repeat collaboration, more franchise-dominated), have its stories become more alike, and how does that relate to how audiences rate the films?

  • 01Is the network more clustered and more hub-dominated in the 2010s than in the 1980s, beyond what random casting would give?
  • 02Are plots within a decade, and within a collaboration community, more similar to each other over time?
  • 03Do films made inside tight circles rate differently from films that bring together people from different communities?

Filter: US feature films, 1980–2024, at least 25,000 IMDb votes, then the 400 most-voted films of each decade. Older films collect fewer votes, so a single vote threshold would over-sample recent decades; fixing 400 per decade makes the five networks the same size. 2020s = 2020–2024 only.

THE SCRIPT

Methods, in three acts

ACT I

The network closes

  • One collaboration network per decade
  • Clustering, degree distribution, hub share
  • Repeat-collaboration rate, returning people
  • Louvain communities and modularity
Check: the same measures after shuffling people across films. Everyone keeps their number of films and every film its cast size (a bipartite null, since each film is a 5-person clique).
ACT II

The stories converge

  • TF-IDF on TMDB plot overviews, character names removed
  • Average plot similarity per decade and per community
  • Top words that characterize each community
  • Sentiment of plots over time
Check: bootstrap over films; plots shuffled across films with the network fixed; hand-labelled sample of film pairs against the cosine score.
ACT III

Do audiences notice?

  • Rating vs. prior ties among a film's people
  • Bridge films (cross-community) vs. in-group films
  • Betweenness of directors vs. their films' ratings
Check: shuffle ratings across films within each decade and compare to the observed effect.
THE REVIEWS

Results so far

ACT I

The club is closing

★★★★☆

Repeat collaborations doubled and more work goes to returning people, well beyond random casting.

ACT II

Same stories, via sequels

★★☆☆☆

Plots grow more alike only because of franchises; outside them, barely any change.

ACT III

Audiences barely notice

★☆☆☆☆

Tight-circle films rate a little lower, but the gap vanishes once genre and franchise are controlled.

ACT I

The network closes

  • Fewer people, same number of films. The 400 films of the 1980s involve 1,233 people; the 2010s only 1,058. The largest connected group grows from 86% to 95% of people.
  • Repeat collaborations doubled: 3.2% of ties in the 1980s were people working together on 2+ films, 6.1% in the 2010s. Random casting gives about 0.4%.
  • Clustering beyond chance grows every decade: z-score against the null rises from 11 (1980s) to 61 (2010s).
  • Returning people take more of the work: credits held by people from the previous decade rise from 42% to 52%.
  • Not more hub-dominated: the top 5% of people hold a flat 18–19% of credits. It is the wider middle of the industry that closes up, not a few stars taking over.
Largest connected group of each decade. Colour = Louvain community, named after its most-credited member; gold lines = pairs who made 2+ films together. Hover a person for their films, click to see their collaborators (the selection carries over when you switch decade), scroll to zoom, drag to pan.

The 2020s look more open on every measure, but they cover only 2020–2024: fewer years to work together again, and recent films have fewer votes. We do not read this as Hollywood reopening.

ACT II

The stories converge?

  • Plots in the 2010s are about 17% more alike than in the 1980s (TF-IDF cosine on plot overviews, character names removed).
  • Without franchise films the rise is only about 3%: the convergence comes from sequels and franchises.
  • Films that share a person have more similar plots, far beyond the shuffled-plots null (z = 7–12 in every decade).
  • In the 2000s and 2010s that gap almost disappears once pairs from the same franchise are left out: "the same people tell the same stories" there mostly means sequels.

Still to come: sentence-embedding version as a second method, similarity and top words per community, and a hand-labelled sample of film pairs.

ACT III

Do audiences notice?

  • Tight-circle films rate 0.1–0.25 points lower than other films in every decade (people who had already worked together on an earlier film).
  • With decade, genre and franchise controlled, the gap is not significant (permutation p = 0.80). Bridge films spanning more communities: +0.03 per community, p = 0.11.
  • Franchise films rate about 0.2 points lower than other films with the same number of votes (p < 0.001).
  • Directors who broker between groups do not make better-rated films: among directors with 2+ films in a decade, betweenness vs. rating ρ = 0.04 (p = 0.51).
